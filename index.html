A short web animation explaining a classic Ramsey number proof

I really enjoyed the talk Recent Results in Ramsey Theory given by Robert Morris at the 2026 ICM.

Define \(R(k)\) as the smallest number such that for any random coloring of edges on a complete graph, there must exist a monochromatic complete induced subgraph (clique) of size \(k\).

Ramsey proved that as \(n\) gets sufficiently large, \(R(k)\) is finite. Erdős and Szekeres then were able to quantify an upper bound on \(R(k)\).

To help myself better understand their proof, I discussed it with a friend afterwards. After thinking, researching, and writing and rewriting a bit more, I felt I understood it much better. Since Ramsey theory is a beautiful and exciting topic, I wanted to share my understanding as well.

Given the algorithm, as described in my web animation, there were two points I was unsure about:

  1. How do we know that the majority set obtained is a clique?
  2. How do we obtain the upper bound \(4^k\)?
